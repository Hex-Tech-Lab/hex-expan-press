The buyer Terms say sales are US-only. The checkout route has no country check (web/app/api/billing/checkout/route.ts, payments/src). The sentence is kept and highlighted until you choose one option.

(a) Keep and enforceIndustry norm: sellers state a territory and enforce it at the payment layer, using the billing country the merchant of record returns, with a block before payment.
Recommendation: choose (a). Make enforcement a go-live gate, not a later task, because the buyer copy promises it now.
Why: the merchant of record already collects billing country, so the build is small; the terms then match the planned US-only launch.
Why not: until the check ships, the sentence is untrue for a non-US buyer who gets through, and the business would be relying on a promise it does not enforce.
(b) Remove the promiseIndustry norm: many digital sellers sell worldwide through a merchant of record that handles tax in each country; naming a territory is optional.
Recommendation: choose (b) only if you are willing to sell worldwide at launch or cannot ship the check before launch. Otherwise choose (a).
Why: removes an unenforced promise at once, with no code change, and the merchant of record still handles tax for any country it bills.
Why not: it departs from the US-only plan, opens the business to non-US buyers and their consumer and tax rules, and conflicts with the founder's US-only planning rule.
