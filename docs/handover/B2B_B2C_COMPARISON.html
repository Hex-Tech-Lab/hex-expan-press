Options behind the main decisions

Each row gives the industry standard, my recommendation, and the reasons for and against it.

Decision Industry standard My recommendation Why yes Why no
1. Revenue share, and "never charged" A stated percentage of net sales, with net sales defined in the agreement (for example, after refunds and taxes), and a statement to the creator. One founder-set percentage with a written net-sales definition, and no creator fee, so "never charged" stays true (terms.html:51). Clear and auditable, and it matches the existing "never charged" line. The number locks economics before launch, and I cannot choose it from the sources. A net-of-refunds definition lowers creator earnings.
2. Who pays creators The merchant of record pays the seller, and the seller pays creators on a stated schedule with a sales statement. ExpanPress pays on a fixed schedule after settlement, with a minimum threshold and a statement with each payout. One payout channel and one schedule for every creator. ExpanPress holds the money between settlement and payout and carries payout and information-return duties. The repo describes manual payouts for Lemon Squeezy and Payhip (payments/README.md:4), and the sources do not say how Paddle payouts work.
3. Refund decider and creator reversal The merchant of record decides refunds, and the seller's share of a refunded sale is reversed against future payouts. The merchant of record decides, as all three columns say. Refunded amounts are netted from the next payout, with a rule for negative balances. It matches the merchant of record's terms, and ExpanPress has no separate refund decision to defend. Creators see clawbacks, and negative balances need a rule of their own.
4. Download links after a refund It varies. Many digital sellers end access on a full refund, while others let access run until expiry. Check the merchant of record's terms. End access on a full refund, but only once revocation is built. Until then the refund copy says links stay valid until expiry and makes no revocation claim. A refunded buyer does not keep the file, and it fits a one-refund-per-sale scope. Revocation is backlog. CLAUDE.md (Tooling Debt) records "Refund and chargeback revocation not implemented (backlog)", so it cannot ship now.
5. Governing law and venue The contract follows the law of the state where the contracting entity is based, and consumers keep mandatory rights where they live. The state of the registered entity, once the founder sets it, with legal review of the venue clause. It is predictable, and it matches where the entity is formed. No source names the state or the entity, so I cannot fill it in.
6. Creator tax forms and information returns Collect IRS Form W-9 from US creators (W-8BEN from non-US creators) before the first payment, and issue information returns where the tax rules require. Collect the form before the first payout, and have a tax adviser confirm the return obligation. It is a standard compliance step. It adds one onboarding step for creators, and the adviser's confirmation is needed first.
7. Licence scope, term and termination A non-exclusive publishing licence for a stated term, a termination notice period, and buyers keeping the licence they already bought. Non-exclusive and term-limited, with a founder-set notice period, and buyer licences surviving termination (terms.html:36; books/legal/terms.html:40). The creator can keep publishing elsewhere, and buyers stay protected. An exclusive licence would be worth more to ExpanPress, but it needs a negotiated deal, and the sources give no price for one.
